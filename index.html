Lamb Mashkool & Stuffed Dates

Lamb Mashkool

A fragrant one-pot rice dish with tender spiced lamb, vegetables and aromatic Gulf spices — a true Gulf feast.

Mashkool is popular across the Gulf. The rice soaks up the rich lamb broth and spices, often finished with fried onions, nuts and raisins. A celebration dish that brings the whole family together.

Stuffed Dates

Soft, sweet dates filled with nuts (often almonds or walnuts) — a simple yet elegant traditional treat.

Dates are a symbol of hospitality in Qatar. Stuffed dates are often served with Arabic coffee to welcome guests and are popular during Ramadan.

Shawarma & Dips Platter

Shawarma

Marinated meat slow-roasted on a vertical spit, thinly sliced and wrapped in soft bread with garlic sauce and pickles.

One of the most popular street foods across the Middle East and very common in Qatar. Juicy, flavourful, and perfect any time of day.

Dips Platter (Mezze)

A colourful spread of creamy hummus, smoky mutabal, labneh and more — perfect for sharing with warm kubz.

Mezze-style dips are a staple of Gulf hospitality. A platter usually includes hummus, moutabbal (roasted eggplant), labneh and pickles, served with fresh Arabic bread for scooping.

Chicken Mashkool & Kubz

Chicken Mashkool

A delicious layered rice dish with spiced chicken, vegetables and aromatic Gulf spices.

Mashkool is popular across the Gulf. The rice is fragrant and colourful, often topped with fried potatoes, raisins and nuts. A true feast on a plate.
Tap for more info

Kubz (Arabic Bread)

Soft, warm flatbread that is essential at almost every Qatari meal — used for scooping stews or wrapping food.

Fresh kubz is baked daily and is a staple of Gulf cuisine. It pairs perfectly with almost every savoury dish and is never wasted in Qatari homes.

Aaqif

Baklava & Qahwa

Baklava

Layers of flaky phyllo pastry filled with chopped nuts and sweetened with honey or syrup — rich, crispy and irresistible.

Baklava is a classic Middle Eastern dessert enjoyed throughout Qatar. Perfect with Arabic coffee or karak tea after a meal.
Tap for more info

Qahwa (Arabic Coffee)

Lightly roasted coffee brewed with cardamom and sometimes saffron — the golden symbol of Gulf hospitality.

Served in small finjaan cups from a traditional dallah pot, qahwa is offered to every guest. UNESCO lists Arabic coffee as Intangible Cultural Heritage. Guests jiggle the cup to show they have had enough.

Fayad

Kanfaroosh & Muhallabiya

Kanfaroosh (Khanfaroosh)

Golden fried saffron and cardamom cakes — crispy on the outside, soft and spongy inside. A traditional Gulf sweet.

Kanfaroosh is flavoured with saffron, cardamom and sometimes rose water. Commonly served with Arabic coffee during gatherings and celebrations in Qatar.
Tap for more info

Muhallabiya

A silky milk pudding flavoured with rose water or orange blossom, topped with crushed pistachios — light, fragrant and refreshing.

Muhallabiya (also called mahalabia) is a classic Middle Eastern dessert. It is chilled until set and often finished with nuts or a drizzle of syrup. Perfect after a rich meal.

Umair

Luqaimat & Harees

Luqaimat

Crispy golden fried dough balls drizzled with sweet date syrup — crunchy outside, soft and fluffy inside.

Luqaimat is one of the most loved desserts in Qatar, especially during Ramadan. Best eaten fresh and hot with date syrup or honey.
Tap for more info

Harees

A smooth, hearty porridge of wheat and meat (lamb or chicken) slow-cooked for hours, finished with a pool of golden ghee.

Harees is one of Qatar’s most treasured traditional dishes, especially during Ramadan and Eid. The wheat and meat are beaten until completely smooth, then topped with ghee.
