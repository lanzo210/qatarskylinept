Lamb Thareed & Lamb Quzi

Lamb Thareed

A hearty traditional stew of tender lamb and vegetables poured over thin regag bread that soaks up all the rich broth.

Thareed is one of the oldest Arab dishes. In Qatar it is especially loved during Ramadan. The bread becomes soft and full of flavour — pure comfort food.

Lamb Quzi (Ghuzi)

Slow-roasted lamb served over fragrant spiced rice with nuts and raisins — a true celebration dish.

Quzi (also called Ghuzi) is often prepared for special occasions and large gatherings. The meat is tender and aromatic, and the rice soaks up all the delicious juices.

Shawarma & Stuffed Dates

Shawarma

Marinated meat slow-roasted on a vertical spit, thinly sliced and wrapped in soft bread with garlic sauce and pickles.

One of the most popular street foods across the Middle East and very common in Qatar. Juicy, flavourful, and perfect any time of day.
Tap for more info

Stuffed Dates

Soft, sweet dates filled with nuts (often almonds or walnuts) — a simple yet elegant traditional treat.

Dates are a symbol of hospitality in Qatar. Stuffed dates are often served with Arabic coffee to welcome guests and are popular during Ramadan.

Chicken Mashkool & Madrouba

Chicken Mashkool

A delicious layered rice dish with spiced chicken, vegetables and aromatic Gulf spices.

Mashkool is popular across the Gulf. The rice is fragrant and colourful, often topped with fried potatoes, raisins and nuts. A true feast on a plate.
Tap for more info

Madrouba

A creamy, spicy porridge made with overcooked rice and shredded chicken, flavoured with turmeric, cardamom and warm spices.

Madrouba means “beaten” because the rice is cooked until soft and mixed until smooth. Comforting and especially loved during Ramadan.

Aaqif

Baklava & Kubz

Baklava

Layers of flaky phyllo pastry filled with chopped nuts and sweetened with honey or syrup — rich, crispy and irresistible.

Baklava is a classic Middle Eastern dessert enjoyed throughout Qatar. Perfect with Arabic coffee or karak tea after a meal.
Tap for more info

Kubz (Arabic Bread)

Soft, warm flatbread that is essential at almost every Qatari meal — used for scooping stews or wrapping food.

Fresh kubz is baked daily and is a staple of Gulf cuisine. It pairs perfectly with almost every savoury dish.

Fayad

Kanfaroosh & Khabees

Kanfaroosh (Khanfaroosh)

Golden fried saffron and cardamom cakes — crispy on the outside, soft and spongy inside. A traditional Gulf sweet.

Kanfaroosh is flavoured with saffron, cardamom and sometimes rose water. Commonly served with Arabic coffee during gatherings and celebrations in Qatar.
Tap for more info

Khabees

A traditional Gulf sweet made from toasted flour, ghee, sugar, cardamom and saffron — soft, rich and aromatic.

Khabees is a favourite breakfast dish in Qatar, especially on Eid mornings. It is often topped with crushed pistachios or almonds and enjoyed with Arabic coffee.

Umair

Luqaimat & Qatayef

Luqaimat

Crispy golden fried dough balls drizzled with sweet date syrup — crunchy outside, soft and fluffy inside.

Luqaimat is one of the most loved desserts in Qatar, especially during Ramadan. Best eaten fresh and hot with date syrup or honey.
Tap for more info

Qatayef

Sweet stuffed pancakes filled with cheese or nuts, then fried or baked and soaked in syrup — a Ramadan favourite.

Qatayef is especially popular during Ramadan. The combination of soft pancake, rich filling and sweet syrup is irresistible.
